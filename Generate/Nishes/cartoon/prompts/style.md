Style guide for the cartoon_photos niche.

Each segment becomes one still frame that visually illustrates what the segment says, drawn in the clean 2D editorial comic style below. Write one prompt per segment in English. The task names frame_language as the language for any rare in-world sign text.

# Global Style

Clean, even 2D comic ink outlines in medium-fine black with smooth closed lines. Warm muted earthy palette: pale dusty blue sky, sandy and ochre earth, olive and sage greens, warm medium wood, muted terracotta and mustard accents. Flat fills with at most one simple darker cel shade along the lower edge and an occasional tiny curved highlight tick on glass or liquid.

Indoor scenes use a flat warm cream wall, a simple counter or wooden table and clean furniture outlines. Pure white is reserved for the white head of the main character and clean physical paper surfaces when part of a scene. Keep each scene on one flat plane with clear brightness separation between the main foreground subject and the uncluttered background so the viewer's eye locks onto the subject in one second.

Describe smooth closed ink outlines, flat matte surfaces and simple shapes. Avoid rough wobbly pen lines, cross-hatching, 3D CGI looks, soft airbrush gradients, bokeh, depth of field and realistic photos.

Do not write any closing style summary sentence at the end of a prompt (no sentence that restates the outlines, palette, finish or art style). The style sentence from preset.md is appended automatically after generation, so every prompt ends on its last concrete scene description.

# Subject, Focus and Literal Visual Communication

Draw the scene, setting, props and concept from the whole Sentence line, using the Before and After lines to follow the story. The >>marked<< fragment is the primary beat: the frame must literally and unmistakably show what that fragment says, while the rest of the sentence supplies the setting and concrete props. A short subtitle fragment rarely shows enough alone, so build the picture from the full sentence.

Focus and clarity rule (anti-mush):
- Show 1 to 2 clear focal objects or subjects per frame on a clean, uncluttered background. Every visual element must directly serve the spoken idea; omit decorative filler and crowded tiny clutter.
- Keep strong brightness and colour contrast between the main focal object and its backdrop so a viewer instantly recognizes the subject in under one second.
- When the narration explains a mechanism, comparison, cause or risk, turn it into a concrete physical object, action, cutaway or visual metaphor rather than an abstract scene or text slide.

# Visual Diversity, Eye-Trace and Shot Scale

Choose each shot by the segment's meaning across four independent axes so consecutive frames never fall into a rigid cycle or repetitive template. Adjacent segments must differ on at least two axes, and the exact same layout mode or camera angle must never repeat back to back:

1. **Composition and shot scale** (pick the mode that best conveys the beat):
- Wide establishing scene: a field, coast, harbor, city street, farm, factory floor or market, with a figure at about 20 percent of image height.
- Medium interaction: a figure at 40 to 60 percent of image height, leaning in, seated, walking or handling a concrete object.
- Three-quarter full figure: a character shown from head to toe at a natural diagonal angle in context.
- Foreground-to-background depth: one large concrete prop in the foreground with the main setting or small figure visible behind it on the same flat 2D plane.
- Macro view of hands or objects: two hands peeling a tuber, a hand holding a crab shell, two specimens compared side by side. Hands and objects may fill most of the frame; a human face never does.
- Overhead flat view: dishes on a table, sorted produce in a tray, tools on a workbench, seen from directly above.
- Cross-section or cutaway: a vertical soil cross-section, an enlarged biological vessel, a cut-open shell or root system (pure visual illustration, clean shapes).
- Illustrated flat map: olive and sage landmasses, crisp coastlines, a dashed terracotta route arrow showing movement or spread (pure visual geography, clean shapes).
- Split comparison: the frame divided vertically into two clean visual panels comparing object A on the left and object B on the right (pure visual contrast).
- Visual metaphor: a brass balance scale tipping toward one object, a chained padlock, circular process arrows, a spotlight cone over a single object, a magnifying glass enlarging a concrete detail, a red warning triangle or a red X over a rejected item.
- Neat arrangement: lined-up crops, shelves stocked with jars, a crate of produce or a small heap on a conveyor belt, kept clean and readable with few tiny items.

2. **Camera angle** (vary naturally across scenes instead of defaulting to flat front-on eye-level):
- Eye-level view, low angle (looking slightly upward to emphasize height, threat or scale), high angle (looking down across a workspace, table or coast), top-down bird's-eye view, three-quarter angle, side profile view, and back view or over-the-shoulder view looking past a figure toward an object or landscape.

3. **Subject placement and Eye-Trace continuity**:
- Keep the primary focal subject inside a readable central or gently shifting zone across adjacent frames so the viewer's eyes never have to jump abruptly between extreme opposite corners on every cut.
- Alternate placement smoothly across segments: center-left, center-right, centered macro, diagonal foreground-to-background, seen from behind, or character-free object/cutaway frames.
- About half or more of all frames work best as pure object, macro, cutaway, map and balance-scale scenes with people and personified characters left out. Add a character only when human action, scale, tasting, confusion, fishing, farming, factory or lab work, or clear physical emotion adds value to the segment.

4. **Emotional flat 2D light temperature and mood palette**:
- Match the flat 2D backdrop and light temperature to the emotional tone of the segment while keeping crisp flat fills and single-layer ink outlines:
  - Tension, threat, paradox or warning: cooler pale dusty slate sky or shadowed indoor cream wall with sharp muted terracotta or deep red focal accents.
  - Discovery, scale or historical drama: warm ochre midday light, rich amber dusk horizon or bright clean warm cream spotlight cone.
  - Calm explanation or breathing room: soft pale dusty-blue sky, warm pale cream backdrop and balanced olive/sage tones.

Never reuse one fixed shot order or sentence opener. Do not start every prompt with the same phrase. Vary the visual mode from clip to clip: never use the same layout mode for three consecutive segments, such as three medium kitchen scenes in a row, and never alternate overhead and macro shots of the same object back to back. Open each prompt with its subject or camera view, for example "Macro view of two hands ...", "Low-angle view of ...", "Overhead flat view of ...", "Over-the-shoulder view past ...", "Vertical soil cross-section ...", "Brass balance scale on a warm cream backdrop ...", "Split vertically down the center ...". When consecutive segments share one sentence, give each segment a different facet of the same idea.

Close-up views are allowed for hands, feet, food, plants, tools, objects and cutaways: write macro view or tight view for them. Close-ups of faces, heads, eyes and portraits are forbidden: show whole figures at normal scale instead.

# Character Canon

Select the character type with the first matching rule:
1. No characters: maps, balance scales, soil or biological cross-sections, standalone object facts, macro and overhead object views.
2. Personified microbe character: gut microbes and personified crops, organs or objects in a metaphor (competition, cooperation, thirst, mechanical resistance).
3. Cartoon adult figure: a named profession, workplace, laboratory, farm, fishing boat, kitchen, market, history, culture or family.
4. Main character: second-person you, a lone traveller, an unnamed observer, a single person at home, solo cooking or tasting, digestive discomfort, confusion, survival or a human-height scale.
5. Otherwise: no characters, a plain 2D visual illustration.

Keep one kind of human character in a frame and never mix human and personified characters in one frame. Plain 2D animals may appear with the main character or a cartoon adult figure and count toward the 4-character total. Draw ingredients, tools and surroundings as plain inanimate items. Use the same character canon for the whole video.

a) Default hero, the main character: a large smooth pure-white round head with a crisp medium-fine black ink outline, small solid black dot eyes, expressive thin curved eyebrows, one mouth expression on the face (choosing either a closed-line mouth, a closed-line smile, an open O-shaped mouth, or a worried wavy mouth), and a plain solid-black stick torso (adding a hat, apron or backpack only if the scene calls for it, with thin black stick arms, simple black stick hands and thin black stick legs). It may carry one simple accessory: a backpack, a chef hat and apron, a farmer hat, a hard hat, a shopping basket or a magnifying glass. Repeat this complete description in every prompt where the main character appears, because each frame is generated independently.

Cartoon adult figure: a proportional 2D figure (head about one fifth of body height) with warm natural skin tones, simple solid-colour hair, small black dot eyes, a small curved-line nose, expressive eyebrows, one mouth expression on the face (a closed-line mouth, a closed-line smile, an open O-shaped mouth, or a worried wavy mouth) and proportional clothed limbs. Keep one fixed outfit per role across the whole video: white lab coat and eyeglasses for a scientist; muted rust plaid flannel shirt, medium-blue denim overalls and brown boots for a farmer; navy sweater, muted yellow rain slicker and rubber boots for a fisherman; muted brown work shirt, tan trousers, brown boots and a tan hard hat for a miner; white jacket and tall white cap for a chef; period tunic and trousers for a historical figure; muted sage sweater and medium-brown trousers for a neutral family member. Repeat the complete face, skin, hair, limb and outfit description of each cartoon adult figure in every prompt where it appears.

Personified character: an active crop, plant, organ part or microbe with small black dot eyes, expressive eyebrows, a closed-line mouth, thin black stick arms and legs, and at most one tiny prop when it enacts a metaphor. Draw every microbe as a personified character rather than plain slime. Keep non-agent tools plain, with no eyes or mouths. Draw real and extinct animals (fish, crabs, trilobites, dinosaurs, prehistoric cats, insects, birds, mammals, burrowing animals) as plain 2D animals with neutral animal features.

Show emotion on the face and through posture and action: choose one mouth shape directly on the face (closed-line smile, open O-shaped mouth, or worried wavy mouth), add squinting happy arc eyes for joy, and place comic signs (sweat drops at the temple, a small question mark or exclamation mark above the head) cleanly beside or above the head. Characters stay silent: no speech bubbles, no dialogue quotes, no microphones, and nobody talks to the camera.

# Text Policy (Pure Visuals; Diegetic In-World Signs Only)

All on-screen captions, numbers, subtitles, timelines, charts and infographics are added later during video editing. Therefore, every frame is a pure visual illustration with no floating or overlaid text of any kind.

Strictly forbidden in every prompt:
- Floating text on the sky, wall or cream backdrop.
- Full-width header bands, title plaques, framed label cards, accent plaques, pointer-arrow text labels, number cards, checklists with text, or standalone text slides.
- Overlay captions, subtitles, callout boxes, dates or statistics pasted over the artwork.

Allowed only as a rare physical part of the real environment (diegetic in-world text):
- A natural physical sign mounted inside the scene itself, such as a storefront shop sign above a market stall or bakery, a wooden harbor sign, or a street plaque, ONLY when the physical setting genuinely calls for it.
- Keep any diegetic sign ultra-short (1 to 2 uppercase words in frame_language, copied from the current segment's Text line if present, ending on a content word, followed by in bold black uppercase lettering). Most frames (over 90 percent) stay completely text-free.

Visual emphasis markers (text-free): two crossed bold red ink strokes making a red X over a rejected object; a red warning triangle with an exclamation mark for a hazard; a clean green checkmark for a working choice.

# Positive Phrasing

Describe only what is visible, as a finished positive scene. When something must look absent, describe the visible state instead: a blank dark screen, clean clear air, a bare black stick torso, neutral animal features. Never write a negative phrase that names the unwanted thing, such as one beginning with the word lacking, because the image model draws the named object.

Safe replacements for common concepts:
- write image height instead of frame height;
- write curves, makes, shapes, creates, covers or lines instead of forms, forming or formed;
- write stocky tan prehistoric cat with long upper canines and a short tail instead of saber-toothed cat;
- write head angled up, leaning forward or mid-step instead of tilting or moving;
- write around, bordering or enclosing instead of framing;
- describe the exact limb pose instead of gesture;
- write cluster, clump, grove, stand or pair instead of group, groups, row of or rows of for trees, rocks, towers and other objects, and write lined-up for crops;
- write two lines meeting at a corner instead of axes;
- write round glass disc or magnifier instead of lens.

Forbidden prompt words to exclude from every prompt: parchment, flag, passport, form, forms, forming, formed, framing, frame height, uniform, emblem, crest, knife, saber, saber-toothed, gesture, gestures, tilting, moving, pan, zoom, track, vector, bold outline, thick outline, heavy outline, sketch, sketchy, 16:9, widescreen, no, not, never, none, render, rendered, rendering, 4k, 8k, uhd, photorealistic, hyperrealistic, masterpiece, trending, octane, unreal engine, lens, 35mm, anamorphic, aperture, focal length, film grain, film still, without, nothing, nobody, cannot, group, groups, crowd, crowds, row of, rows of, portrait.

# Safety and Content Rules

Show adults only; children, babies and young people are forbidden. Weapons are forbidden in every frame: use a vegetable peeler, wooden spoon, spatula or garden trowel instead of blades. Blood, wounds, injuries, bruises, physical pain or agony, corpses and any harm, mistreatment or cruelty to animals are strictly forbidden: whenever a segment touches on illness, danger or harsh conditions, show intact unharmed subjects, a clean educational diagram, or a calm visual metaphor (such as a warning triangle or balance scale) instead. Speech is silent: write the expression itself (smile, squint, O-shaped mouth, worried mouth) and never a speech verb, shouted word, or a talking mouth.

Include at most 4 characters in total per frame, counting humans and animals together. When two to four adults appear, describe each adult one by one, or write two adult characters or three adult characters. Avoid plural group nouns such as people, men, women, farmers, workers, villagers, crowds or groups.

Object arrangements are allowed when they are neat and readable: lined-up crops, shelves stocked with jars, a crate of produce, a small heap on a conveyor belt. Keep each kind of object to one clear grouping and avoid clutter of tiny items.

Every frame shows at least one concrete foreground focal object, cutaway or character; bare empty sky-and-ground landscapes are forbidden. When a segment names absence or an abstract concept (empty land, heat, extinction, an era, a transition), show a concrete object, cross-section, thermometer, fossil, clock, compass or visual marker, or keep the main character examining a concrete object from the segment. When a segment names a body part, show the whole character pointing or touching that part; hands alone appear only in a macro view of handling an object.

Exclude @handles, watermarks, video player UI, random ducks or ponds unless the segment names them, and mixed-language garbage text.

When consecutive segments share the same subject, vary at least two elements from frame to frame: the layout, the focal object or prop, the character pose or action, and the setting. Avoid repeated identical backdrops.

# Choosing the Frame

Pick the layout by the segment's main idea, checking in this order:
1. Direct comparison or contrast: split visual panels, a brass balance scale tipping toward one object, or an equals sign with a red X (pure visuals).
2. Process, sequence, cycle or a choice of paths: two to three linked clean visual steps with black ink arrows.
3. Geography, origin or a trade route: an illustrated flat map with a dashed terracotta arrow (pure visual geography).
4. Underground growth, roots or internal biology: a clean cross-section or cutaway.
5. Daily life, survival, cooking, fishing, farming, lab work, a market or a personified metaphor: a wide or medium scene with a character, following the character canon.
6. Standalone concept, number or risk: a concrete focal object on a clean contrasting backdrop (with a red warning triangle, magnifying glass or spotlight cone when highlighting a threat or key detail).

# Final Check

Before returning each prompt, verify every item: one finished scene with 1 to 2 clear focal objects, a cutaway or a character on an uncluttered contrasting background; literal visual depiction of the marked beat; the shot scale and opener differ from the previous prompt; a character appears only when the character canon selects it, with at most 4 characters in total; the complete visual description of every main character, cartoon adult figure and personified character is written in full inside this prompt; zero floating or overlaid text, label cards, plaques or header bands (only a rare physical in-world storefront or street sign if genuinely part of the setting); the prompt uses positive wording and contains no forbidden words and no quotation marks; no closing style summary sentence is written at the end.
