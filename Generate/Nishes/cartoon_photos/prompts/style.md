Style guide for the cartoon_photos niche.

Each segment becomes one still frame that illustrates what the segment says, drawn in the clean 2D editorial comic style below. Write one prompt per segment in English. The task names frame_language as the language for all on-frame text.

# Global Style

Clean, even 2D comic ink outlines in medium-fine black with smooth closed lines. Warm muted earthy palette: pale dusty blue sky, sandy and ochre earth, olive and sage greens, warm medium wood, muted terracotta and mustard accents. Flat fills with at most one simple darker cel shade along the lower edge and rare tiny curved highlight ticks on glass or liquid.

Cards, comparisons, charts and diagrams sit on a warm pale cream backdrop rather than pure #FFFFFF, reserving pure white solely for small foreground title plaques, paper label cards, callout bubbles and the white head of type A.

Outdoor 2D scenes use four clean flat layers: pale dusty blue sky with two to three puffy white clouds, distant muted olive-sage hills, flat tan dirt or olive grass with sparse tiny grass tufts, and an optional horizontal brown soil cross-section at the bottom dotted with tiny tan pebbles and thin roots. Indoor 2D scenes use a flat warm cream wall, simple counter or wooden table, and clean furniture outlines. Everything sits on a flat single plane.

Prompt writers describe smooth closed ink outlines, flat matte surfaces and simple shapes, avoiding rough wobbly pen lines, cross-hatching, 3D rendering, soft airbrush gradients, bokeh, depth of field and photorealism.

# Safety and Content Rules

Never show children, babies or young people: show adults only. No weapons in any frame: use a vegetable peeler, wooden spoon, spatula or garden trowel instead of blades. No speech of any kind: characters stay silent with closed-line mouths; exclude open mouths, laughing, shouting, screaming and speech verbs such as says, shouts, talks or yells. Keep hands and fingers far away from fire, flames, stoves and candles. Frame every shot wide or medium-wide; exclude close-up faces. Include at most 4 characters total per frame, counting humans and animals together.

Crowds, piles, rows, shelves and stacks are forbidden: show at most 4 separate objects, described individually one by one. Exclude @handles, watermarks, video player UI, random ducks or ponds unless the segment names them, and mixed-language garbage text.

Every frame must show a concrete visible object or landscape; a shadow or ground ellipse beneath empty space is forbidden. When a segment is abstract (an era, a transition or a concept), show a characteristic landscape, rock, clock, compass or concrete object from the segment. When a segment names a body part (arm, shoulder, hand, stomach or head), show the whole character in a wide or medium-wide shot pointing at or touching that part; isolated body parts are forbidden.

When consecutive segments share the same subject, vary the setting, pose, prop and framing from frame to frame, avoiding repeated identical cards or repeated identical backdrops.

Describe the finished still frame in plain positive English wording free of quotation marks, taking objects, characters, settings and on-frame text from the Text line of the segment alone.
Forbidden prompt words to exclude from every prompt: parchment, flag, passport, form, uniform, emblem, crest, knife, vector, bold outline, thick outline, heavy outline, sketch, sketchy, 16:9, widescreen, no, not, never, none.

# Character Canon

a) Default hero — type A, the white-head black stickman. Refer to this character consistently as the main character when type A applies. Description: the main character has a large smooth pure-white round head with a crisp medium-fine black ink outline, small solid black dot eyes, expressive thin curved eyebrows, a simple closed-line mouth, and a plain thin solid-black stick body with thin black stick arms, simple black stick hands and thin black stick legs, completely free of clothing or headwear. Add one named role garment only when the current segment explicitly names cooking or farming, and include only that named item.

b) Full 2D cartoon human — type B. Proportional adult comic figure (head about one fifth of body height) with warm natural skin tones, simple solid-color hair, small black dot eyes, a small curved-line nose, expressive eyebrows, a closed-line mouth and proportional clothed limbs. Keep the exact same face design and one fixed outfit per role across the whole video: white lab coat and eyeglasses for a scientist; muted rust plaid flannel shirt, medium-blue denim overalls and brown boots for a farmer; muted brown work shirt, tan trousers, brown boots and tan hard hat for a miner; muted rust vest, tan trousers and hard hat for a factory or quarry worker; white jacket and tall white cap for a chef; period tunic and trousers for a historical figure. When a group or family needs a neutral outfit, use one fixed colour set: muted sage sweater and medium-brown trousers.

c) Anthropomorphic mascot — type C. Active crop, plant, organ part or microbe drawn with small black dot eyes, expressive eyebrows, a closed-line mouth and thin black stick arms and legs, plus an optional tiny prop when enacting a metaphor. Draw every microbe as a type C character with dot eyes and a closed-line mouth rather than plain slime. Keep non-agent objects such as a small digger or a tool as plain inanimate items free of eyes or mouths.

Character selection order (first match wins):
1. Zero characters: geography, maps, charts, balance scales, factor grids, definitions, botanical or soil cross-sections, molecular diagrams, standalone object facts.
2. Mascot (type C): gut microbes and active personified plant or object metaphors (competition, cooperation, thirst, mechanical resistance).
3. Full 2D cartoon human (type B): a named or implied profession (scientist, farmer, miner, worker, chef), workplace, laboratory, farm, restaurant, history, culture, family or group of more than one person.
4. White-head stickman (type A): second-person you, a lone traveller, an unnamed observer, and any single person in home life, solo cooking, tasting, solo shopping, digestive discomfort, confusion, survival or human-height scale.
5. Otherwise: zero characters, plain 2D concept visual or card.

Sticky rule: if the previous frame was a human type (A or B) and the current segment's first-match type is also human, keep the previous type. Change human type only on a clear change of role (for example home cooking to a factory). Keep A or B fixed throughout the same scene and the same home episode.

Single type per frame: keep exactly one character mode per frame; leave mixed types out of every frame. Draw all surrounding crops, ingredients and objects as plain inanimate 2D items whenever type A or type B appears. Use the same character canon for the whole video.

Reaction signals: show reaction and emotion through posture, action, angled eyebrows, a closed curve or wavy line mouth, tiny sweat drops near the temple, or a small question or exclamation mark above the head.

# On-Frame Text, Banners and Visual Markers

Keep most frames completely free of text, plaques and labels. Add text inside the frame solely for these five cases: numbers, dates and measurements (for example 255 MILLION YEARS or 9 M LONG); proper names, eras and places (for example PANGAEA or CARBONIFEROUS); direct two-item comparisons (A | B); map labels; and chart labels. The language of all on-frame text is frame_language.

Subtitle fragments, verbs, pronouns or prepositions taken as text (such as IN TIME, OF THE PIT, YOU SPOT, FOR A SECOND or THE END) are forbidden. Empty plaques and blank floating label cards are forbidden: every plaque or banner sits docked at the top center and holds real uppercase text inside it.

Every on-frame text item is a real phrase of 1 to 4 words taken whole from the current segment's subtitle, ending on a content word, or labels for one object using one word for each part, at most 4 parts. Two text modes apply: a) standalone number, era or concept cards carry exactly one text phrase per frame; b) comparison, map and chart layouts carry one main title plus the short labels or dates that the layout requires. Infographic labels count as text and must be real words from the segment, leaving invented labels and mixed-language text out. Separate text-only slides are at most 1 in 5 clips.

Visual text carriers matching the reference style: bold uppercase black hand lettering centered on the top sky or cream backdrop, white rectangular title plaques or banners framed in thin black ink, small beige or white label cards on maps and charts, and thin curved black ink pointer arrows. Accent numbers or headings may use muted red or gold with a crisp black border.

Emphasis markers: two crossed bold red ink strokes forming a red X over a rejected object or over an equals sign; a red warning triangle with an exclamation mark for hazards; a clean green checkmark for working choices.

# Typical Layouts

Choose the one layout that fits the segment:

a) Narrative 2D scene: four-layer outdoor landscape or simple indoor room with type A, type B or type C in one clear action, kept free of text unless a specific number, proper name or era requires one top banner.
b) Soil or biological cross-section: flat upper ground with plants above a clean horizon line and a warm brown earth cutaway below showing roots and separate tubers, or an enlarged cutaway of a vessel or organ.
c) Comparison A | B: warm cream backdrop split vertically down the center by one solid or dashed black line, with white framed title plaques above each side and two contrasting scenes or objects.
d) Contrast with symbol or scale: warm cream card or outdoor strip with two separate objects or mascots facing an equals sign, a crossed red X over an equals sign, opposing arrows or a brass balance scale.
e) Process flow, cycle or crossroads: two to four separate mini-illustrations linked left-to-right or in a cycle by clean black ink arrows, or a dirt path forking left and right toward two different destinations.
f) Cartoon chart or checklist card: warm cream card with hand-drawn black X and Y axes showing two to three colored bars or two curve lines with short labels, or an illustrated clipboard checklist with green checkmarks.
g) Illustrated flat map: olive-tan and sage landmasses with crisp dark coastlines over a pale dusty-blue sea, a dashed terracotta route arrow, small icons and short uppercase labels.
h) Concept, number or era card: warm cream backdrop with one prominent central object or landscape on a soft ground shadow and one bold uppercase title at the top.
i) Arrow or ruler timeline: warm cream backdrop with a clean horizontal arrow or pale tan ruler ticks highlighting one active date or step in muted red.

# Choosing the Frame

Check the segment in this order and use the first layout that fits:
1. Direct two-item comparison or contrast: layout c or d.
2. Process, sequence, cycle, cause and effect or choice of paths: layout e.
3. Quantitative chart, factor breakdown or criteria list: layout f.
4. Geography, origin or trade route: layout g.
5. Underground growth, roots, composition or internal biology: layout b.
6. Dates, numbers, eras or time span: layout h or i.
7. Narrative action, survival, cooking, farming, lab work, market scene or personified crop or microbe metaphor: layout a, applying the Character Canon order and sticky rule.
8. Standalone concept or transition: layout a or h with a concrete visible object or characteristic landscape from the segment.

# Final Check

Before returning each prompt, verify every item: one finished static wide or medium-wide scene with one concrete visible object or landscape; single character type following the canon order and sticky rule; plain stick body for type A unless cooking or farming is named; fixed role outfit for type B; microbes drawn as type C and non-agent tools kept plain; whole figure shown when naming body parts; varied framing across consecutive segments; at most 4 characters total (adults only, closed-line mouths, zero speech verbs); at most 4 separate objects described one by one; on-frame text solely for numbers, dates, measurements, proper names, comparisons, maps or charts, in frame_language, 1 to 4 words per text item, matching text-mode limits (exactly one text phrase per frame on standalone cards, or one main title plus short layout labels on comparisons, maps and charts); warm cream or layered 2D background; the closing style sentence is present; and the prompt text is pure positive wording free of quotation marks and forbidden words.
