Style guide for the cartoon_photos niche.

Each segment becomes one still frame: one scene, one action, drawn in the flat cartoon style of reference 1. Every prompt uses at least one reference from the refs list of its segment. Reference 1 is the base style and is added to most segments. Reference 6 is the main character and is added after the base style whenever the character appears. Choose the reference that matches the content of the segment, and use only reference numbers that appear in the refs list.

Reference roles:
- ref_1 (base style): a flat landscape with a bright sky, a green grass line, brown soil layers and grey rock below, with bold dark outlines and flat colour fills. Use it for any scene with ground, soil, rock, sky or water.
- ref_2 (on-frame text): a white background with one short red handwritten phrase and a small hourglass icon. Use it for any segment that shows text on the frame.
- ref_3 (labelled diagram): a grey rock with a green top, rising blue bubbles and one handwritten label with an arrow. Use it for an explanatory diagram with one label.
- ref_4 (timeline): a horizontal black arrow with evenly spaced circles, a dot at the left end, a small label under each end and a red pin above the line. Use it for time spans or a sequence of periods.
- ref_5 (comparison): two panels side by side, divided by a dashed line, each with a title above it and a short label inside. Use it for comparing two situations.
- ref_6 (main character): a plain round white head with a simple expression, a blue short-sleeved shirt, blue jeans and white shoes, standing straight, full body, on a white background. Use it whenever the character appears.

Visual style: bold dark outlines around every shape, flat colour fills with at most one simple shadow, simple readable shapes, and a limited bright palette of sky blue, green, brown, grey and orange. Diagrams sit on a plain white or pale background. Keep surfaces flat, with simple lighting drawn as shapes only.

Text on the frame: write it in the on-frame language named in the task. Use handwritten capital letters, large enough to read at a glance, and keep it to one short phrase. The default colour is black. On a dark background use white. Use red only for the most important word or number. Any other colour is allowed when it stays easy to read.

Crossing out: draw two red diagonal strokes over the scene, like brush strokes, to show that something is missing, wrong or a myth. Name the crossed-out element in the prompt and place it in the scene, for example a person with equipment and two red diagonal strokes over the scene.

Prompt form: write one or two plain sentences that describe exactly what is visible: the place, the single person and what they do, and any object, label or diagram. End every prompt with this exact sentence: Flat 2D style, bold outlines, flat colour fills.

Label rule: when a segment uses references, write one tag for each reference in its refs list. The tags follow the ascending order of the refs list: <image1> is the lowest number in refs, <image2> the next, and so on. Use each tag at least once, and only those tags.

Final check before each prompt is returned: one scene, one main subject, one person at most, plain props only, the face shown whole and small in the scene, mood shown only through visible actions, describing only the scene itself, quotation marks absent, the fixed ending in place, and at least one reference used.
