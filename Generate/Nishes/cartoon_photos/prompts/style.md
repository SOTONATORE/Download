Style guide for the cartoon_photos niche.

Each segment becomes one still frame that illustrates what the segment says, matching the clean 2D editorial comic style below. Write one prompt per segment in English. The task names the frame language for on-frame text.

# Global Style

Clean, even 2D comic ink outlines in medium-fine black with smooth closed lines. Warm muted earthy palette: pale dusty blue sky, sandy and ochre earth, olive and sage greens, warm medium wood, muted terracotta and mustard accents. Flat fills with at most one simple darker cel shade along the lower edge and rare tiny curved highlight ticks on glass or liquid.

Cards, comparisons, charts and diagrams sit on a warm pale cream backdrop, reserving pure white for small foreground title plaques, paper label cards, callout bubbles and the white head of type A.

Outdoor 2D scenes use four clean flat layers: pale dusty blue sky with two to three puffy white clouds, distant muted olive-sage hills, flat tan dirt or olive grass with sparse tiny grass tufts, and an optional horizontal brown soil cross-section at the bottom dotted with tiny tan pebbles and thin roots. Indoor 2D scenes use a flat warm cream wall, simple counter or wooden table, and clean furniture outlines. Everything sits on a flat single plane.

Instruction for the model writing prompts: write positive visual descriptions using smooth closed ink outlines, flat matte surfaces and simple shapes. Exclude sketchy or wobbly pen lines, cross-hatching, 3D rendering, soft airbrush gradients, bokeh, depth of field and photorealism.

# Safety and Content Rules

Adults only in every human frame; exclude children, babies and young people. Weapons are excluded from every frame; use a vegetable peeler, wooden spoon, spatula or garden trowel instead of blades. Characters stay silent with closed-line mouths; exclude open mouths, laughing, shouting, screaming and speech verbs such as says, shouts, talks or yells. Keep hands and fingers far away from fire, flames, stoves and candles. Every shot is wide or medium-wide; exclude close-up faces. Include at most 4 characters total per frame, counting humans and animals together.

Crowds, piles, rows, shelves and stacks are forbidden: show at most 4 separate objects, described individually one by one. Exclude @handles, watermarks, video player UI, random ducks or ponds unless the segment explicitly names them, and mixed-language text.

Prompt wording rules: describe the finished still frame in plain English wording free of quotation marks and free of negations such as no, not, never, without or none. Exclude the words parchment, flag, passport, form, uniform, emblem, crest, knife, vector, bold outline, thick outline, heavy outline, sketch, sketchy, 16:9 and widescreen; use shape instead of form, cream card instead of parchment, marker tag instead of flag, and role outfit instead of uniform.

Objects, characters, settings and on-frame text come from the Text line of the segment alone.

# Character Canon

Choose one single character mode per frame by checking the rules below in order, stopping at the first match:
1. No character: geography, maps, charts, balance scales, factor grids, definitions, botanical or soil cross-sections, molecular diagrams, standalone object facts.
2. Mascot (type C: anthropomorphic crop, plant or microbe with black dot eyes and thin stick limbs): gut microbes and active personified plant metaphors (competition, cooperation, thirst, mechanical resistance).
3. Full 2D cartoon human (type B: proportional adult comic figure in role outfit): a named or implied profession, workplace, laboratory, farm, restaurant, history, culture, family or group of buyers.
4. White-head stickman (type A: smooth round white head, thin solid-black stick body): an unnamed person in solo home cooking, tasting, solo shopping, digestive discomfort, confusion, human-height scale.
5. Otherwise: no character, plain 2D concept visual or card.

Sticky rule: if the previous frame was a human type (A or B) and the current segment's first-match type is also human, keep the previous type. Change human type only on a clear change of role (for example home cooking to a factory). Never switch A to B or back inside the same scene or the same home episode.

Single type per frame: never mix types in one frame. Keep all surrounding crops, ingredients and objects as plain unfaced 2D items whenever type A or type B appears. Use the exact same character design across the whole video.

a) Default hero — type A, the white-head black stickman. Refer to this character uniformly as the main character when type A applies. Description: the main character has a large smooth pure-white round head with a crisp medium-fine black ink outline, small solid black dot eyes, expressive thin curved eyebrows, a simple closed-line mouth, and a thin solid-black stick body with thin black stick arms, simple black stick hands and thin black stick legs. Add only a simple grey kitchen apron, white chef hat or straw hat when the role needs one, with the stick limbs and body kept unchanged.

b) Full 2D cartoon human — type B. Proportional adult comic human (head about one fifth of body height) with warm natural skin tones, simple solid-color hair, small black dot eyes, a small curved-line nose, expressive eyebrows, a closed-line mouth and proportional clothed limbs. Match the role outfit to the segment: plaid flannel shirt and blue denim overalls for a farmer, white coat and eyeglasses for a scientist, vest and hard hat for a factory worker, chef jacket and toque for a chef, or simple muted sweater and trousers for an everyday adult. Keep the exact same face design across all roles.

c) Anthropomorphic mascot — type C. An active plant, crop, organ part or microbe drawn with small black dot eyes, expressive eyebrows, a closed-line mouth and thin black stick arms and legs, plus an optional tiny prop such as a chef hat, sleeping cap or gloves when enacting a metaphor. Keep inanimate objects plain and unfaced on neutral factual diagrams.

Reaction signals (speech excluded): show reaction and emotion through angled eyebrows, a closed curve or wavy line mouth, tiny sweat drops near the temple, a small question or exclamation mark above the head, or tiny impact ticks around an emphasized item.

# On-Frame Text, Banners and Visual Markers

Text inside the frame is allowed and is part of the style, used for title cards, key numbers, comparison headings, map or chart labels and stamps. Most frames carry no text: add text only when a title, comparison heading, number or diagram label directly clarifies the segment. The language of all on-frame text is frame_language, as named in the task.

Every on-frame text item is a real phrase of 1 to 4 words taken whole from the current segment's subtitle, ending on a content word, or labels for one object using one word for each part, at most 4 parts. Two text modes apply: a) standalone title or concept cards carry exactly one text phrase per frame; b) comparison, process, map and chart layouts carry one main title plus the short labels or dates that the layout requires. Infographic labels count as text and must be real words from the segment, never invented words or mixed-language text. Separate text-only slides are at most 1 in 5 clips.

Visual text carriers matching the reference style: bold uppercase black hand lettering centered on the top sky or cream backdrop, white rectangular title plaques or banners framed in thin black ink, small white paper label cards or wooden stake tags, rounded white callout or thought bubbles framed in black ink (with characters silent and closed-mouthed), and thin curved black ink pointer arrows. Accent words or numbers may use muted red or gold with a crisp black border.

Emphasis markers: two crossed bold red ink strokes forming a red X over a rejected object or over an equals sign; a red warning triangle with an exclamation mark for hazards; a clean green checkmark for working choices.

# Typical Layouts

Choose the one layout that fits the segment:

a) Narrative 2D scene: four-layer outdoor landscape or simple indoor room with type A, type B or type C in one clear action, optionally topped by one short uppercase title or plaque.
b) Soil or biological cross-section: flat upper ground with plants above a clean horizon line and a warm brown earth cutaway below showing roots and separate tubers, or an enlarged cutaway of a vessel or organ with clean black pointer labels.
c) Comparison A | B: warm cream backdrop split vertically down the center by one solid or dashed black line, with white framed title plaques above each side and two contrasting scenes or objects.
d) Contrast with symbol or scale: warm cream card or outdoor strip with two separate objects or mascots facing an equals sign, a crossed red X over an equals sign, opposing arrows or a brass balance scale.
e) Process flow, cycle or crossroads: two to four separate mini-illustrations linked left-to-right or in a cycle by clean black ink arrows, or a dirt path forking left and right toward two different destinations.
f) Cartoon chart or checklist card: warm cream card with hand-drawn black X and Y axes showing two to three colored bars or two curve lines with short labels, or an illustrated clipboard checklist with green checkmarks.
g) Illustrated flat map: olive-tan and sage landmasses with crisp dark coastlines over a pale dusty-blue sea, a dashed terracotta route arrow, small icons and short uppercase labels.
h) Concept title card: warm cream backdrop with one prominent central object illustration on a soft oval ground shadow and one bold uppercase title at the top.
i) Arrow or ruler timeline: warm cream backdrop with a clean horizontal arrow or pale tan ruler ticks highlighting one active date or step in muted red.

# Choosing the Frame

Check the segment in this order and use the first layout that fits:
1. Contrast or comparison of two items or outcomes: layout c or d.
2. Process, sequence, cycle, cause and effect or choice of paths: layout e.
3. Quantitative growth, factor breakdown or criteria list: layout f.
4. Geography, origin or trade route: layout g.
5. Underground growth, roots, composition or internal biology: layout b.
6. Dates or time span: layout i.
7. Narrative human action, cooking, farming, lab work, market scene or personified crop metaphor: layout a, applying the Character Canon priority and sticky rules.
8. Standalone definition or key concept: layout h.

# Final Check

Before returning each prompt, verify every item: one finished static wide or medium-wide scene with one action; single character type following the priority order and sticky rule; at most 4 characters total (adults only, closed-line mouths, zero speech verbs); at most 4 separate objects described one by one with zero crowds, piles, rows, shelves or stacks; objects and text come strictly from the segment subtitle in frame_language, 1 to 4 words per text item, matching text-mode limits (exactly one text phrase per frame on standalone cards, or one main title plus short layout labels on comparisons, maps and charts); warm cream or layered 2D background; the closing style sentence is present; and the prompt text is pure positive wording free of quotation marks, negations and forbidden words.
